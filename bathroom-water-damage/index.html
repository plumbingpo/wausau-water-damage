Bathroom Water Damage Help in Wausau

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Wausau, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
What We Do

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
