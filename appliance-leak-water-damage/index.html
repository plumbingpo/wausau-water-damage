Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Wausau

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Wausau, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Standpipes that clog, or the drain hose from a washer coming loose from one.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Adjoining Rooms

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Flooring and Subfloor

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Coverage

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • How many rooms the water reached, including any ceiling below an upstairs laundry.
  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • If the flooring and cabinets have to come out or can be dried where they are.
  • The time that passed before anyone found the leak, which matters most for slow drips at the back of a unit.

What Happens After You Call
