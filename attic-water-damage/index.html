Attic Water Damage in Wausau

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. For Wausau homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Ceilings upstairs with brownish stains or rings that grow following storms
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Air Conditioner Drips

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.
