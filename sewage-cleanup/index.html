A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Wausau, Wausau Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs You Need Sewage Cleanup in Wausau

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
