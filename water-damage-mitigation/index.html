The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. It is not the repair. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Wausau, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. Each is its own phase of the job. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's what you can expect when you choose Wausau Restoration:

1

Stop the Water Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
  • Downspouts should empty well clear of the foundation, and gutters need to stay free of debris.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
