How Green Bay Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Green Bay homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. If you find something in your Green Bay home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

The First Hour After Water Damage in Green Bay

The hour between finding water and the crew arriving is when a homeowner in Green Bay can do the most good. Safety comes before saving anything. Keep out of water that touches sockets, appliances or wiring, and only cut the power if the breaker box is in a dry place you can get to safely. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Next, stop the source. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. Then move what you can carry: rugs, electronics, papers, and small furniture go to a dry room. Put foil or plastic under the legs of heavy furniture so the wood does not stain the carpet.

Before mopping, record the damage on your phone, since an insurance company will ask what it looked like at the start. If the water is clean and shallow, blot what you can, and let our Green Bay crew handle everything else.

Drying Out a Green Bay Property: What to Expect

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Green Bay property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. Most homes in Green Bay reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

Common Water Damage Issues in Green Bay

In Green Bay, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Green Bay began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
