Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Wausau Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Wausau

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
