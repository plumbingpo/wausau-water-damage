Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Wausau Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Warning Signs

  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth Behind the Paint

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
