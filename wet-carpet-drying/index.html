Wet Carpet Drying: What Wausau Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Wausau homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Warning Signs

  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
