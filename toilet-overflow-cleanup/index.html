Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Wausau

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

Our Wausau crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
How We Fix It

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Identify the Category

We find out whether the water came from the supply side or the bowl and drain, then set up the right protection and containment before any cleanup begins.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Drying and Final Readings

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • A main sewer line blockage that backs waste up through the lowest toilet.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Bathroom flooring material, since some floors dry in place and others must be taken up.
  • How long the water sat before extraction and cleanup began.
  • Whether the overflow stayed in the bathroom or reached carpet, closets or a hall outside it.
  • If the bathroom is on an upper floor, the wet ceiling or walls underneath add to the job.
