Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Wausau

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

Our Process

How We Handle Roof Leak Water Damage

1

Map Where Water Went

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

2

Cover and Protect

If the roof is still open to the weather, we can install a temporary tarp or coordinate with your roofer. Anything sitting beneath the damage, including the flooring, gets protected too.

After removing the insulation and drywall that are past saving, our crew dries the attic and lower rooms with air movers and dehumidifiers and tracks moisture levels until the wood is dry.

Where it is called for, we apply an antimicrobial to the bare framing. After the roofer has finished, new insulation goes in, fresh drywall is hung and finished, and the ceiling is primed and painted to match.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Attic Insulation

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Wall Cavities

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Roof Framing

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Rot in these members weakens the structure that holds the roof up.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.
